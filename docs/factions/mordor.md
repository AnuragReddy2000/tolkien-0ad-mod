# Mordor

## Identity

Mordor is an industrial darkness faction. It values numbers, economy, attrition, and territorial corruption.

## Core mechanics

### Snaga Slaves
Snaga Slaves are the primary workers. Their presence should shape early-game economy and force the opponent to respond to worker protection and harassment.

### Blighted Desolation
Enemy workers gathering within Mordor-controlled territory suffer a gather-rate penalty. This is a strategic territorial effect, not a farm-destruction mechanic. It is strongest near major Mordor structures and least effective far from Mordor territory.

### Crebain and Wargs
Crebain provide reconnaissance and sight. Wargs provide harassment and pursuit, especially against workers and isolated targets.

### All-Seeing Eye
The Barad-dûr reveal is powerful but should be a long-cycle effect. It reveals the entire map briefly and then continues with a large moving search radius. The long cooldown prevents it from being a constant utility button.

## Strengths

- cheap mass production
- strong pressure and attrition
- warg and crebain scouting power
- oppressive territorial presence

## Weaknesses

- weaker in direct elite fights than armies built around quality
- vulnerable to cavalry, anti-large units, and static defensive play
- worker disruption can damage the economy sharply

## Structures

- dark fortresses; Orc and warg production; worker structures; towers; siege support; wonder site.

## Units

- Snaga workers; Orcs; Orc archers; cavalry and raiding units; heavy siege and troll-like support; dark elite infantry.

## Heroes

- Sauron; Gothmog; Khamûl. Khamûl leads scouting and raiding support. Saruman is reserved for alliance or scenario use instead of a standard Mordor hero.

## Wonder

Barad-dûr remains a major late-game information and battlefield control tool. The effect should be strong and noticeable but not permanent map control.

## Counterplay

Disrupt the worker economy, force Mordor to fight in a clean battle, and deny it the temporal advantages that let the attrition engine function.
