# Civilizations

Each civilization is a different answer to a strategy-game question: what does this society value, and what trade-off follows from that value? The game rules are designed to remain understandable even if you have never read Tolkien.

**Playable-data status:** Doriath, Lindon, Mordor, Moria, and Númenor are selectable in the current mod. The remaining entries are design concepts only.

## Doriath — the hidden woodland realm

Doriath is a Sindarin Elven kingdom in the ancient land of Beleriand, ruled by Thingol and protected by Melian's supernatural boundary, the Girdle. Its design turns that idea of a guarded forest into concealment, ambush, and area control. Doriath is strongest when it chooses the ground and weakest when forced into an exposed, direct fight.

[Read the Doriath design](factions/doriath.md).

## Lindon — the western havens

Lindon is the coastal refuge of the Elves in the west of Middle-earth, associated with the Grey Havens and the shipwright Círdan. Its small, costly army, strong ships, and careful defensive craft model a people who prize skill and endurance over rapid replacement. It rewards planning around the coast rather than fielding the largest force.

[Read the Lindon design](factions/lindon.md).

## Lothlórien — the guarded forest

Lothlórien is an Elven realm of the great forest, protected by Galadriel and Celeborn. Its concept focuses on observation, ranged strength, and defense shaped by woodland terrain. The design aims to make vision and positioning meaningful without making forest cover an automatic victory.

[Read the Lothlórien design](factions/lothlorien.md).

## Moria — the Dwarrowdelf beneath the mountains

Khazad-dûm, later called Moria, is a vast Dwarven city and mine beneath the Misty Mountains. Its design expresses that underground stronghold through durable infantry, deep resource workings, and linked tunnels. Moria can defend valuable ground exceptionally well, but has no cavalry and is less effective when drawn into open terrain.

[Read the Moria design](factions/moria.md).

## Erebor — the Lonely Mountain

Erebor is the Dwarven kingdom beneath the Lonely Mountain, renowned for its craft and mineral wealth. Its concept makes metal and engineering central to its economy and military. The intended trade-off is powerful equipment and deliberate investment rather than effortless expansion.

[Read the Erebor design](factions/erebor.md).

## Iron Hills — disciplined Dwarven formations

The Iron Hills are a Dwarven realm east of Erebor, known for coming to its aid in battle. Their design emphasizes compact formations, shielded troops, and field engineering. Heavy units are dependable when organized, but slower movement gives opponents room to choose when to engage.

[Read the Iron Hills design](factions/iron-hills.md).

## Númenor — the island kingdom of Men

Númenor was an island kingdom of Men whose people became famed for seafaring, construction, and military power before the island's fall. The design focuses on strong ships, coastal infrastructure, and disciplined, expensive troops. It is a high-quality force, not an unlimited one.

[Read the Númenor design](factions/numenor.md).

## Rohan — the horse-lords

Rohan is a kingdom of the plains whose people are closely tied to horses and mounted warfare. Its concept uses mobility, scouting, and well-timed charges as the core of its army. Open ground enables those strengths; restrictive terrain and prepared defenses challenge them.

[Read the Rohan design](factions/rohan.md).

## Gondor and Arnor — the successor kingdoms

Gondor and Arnor were successor kingdoms founded by the Dúnedain, descendants of Númenor. Their design branches from a shared foundation: a player can lean toward ranger mobility or toward fortified defense. The choice is meant to express two different responses to the same inherited legacy.

[Read the Gondor and Arnor design](factions/gondor-arnor.md).

## Harad and Umbar — southern and maritime powers

Harad describes lands and peoples south of Gondor; Umbar is a major southern port and naval power. Their combined concept explores raiding, trade, ships, and powerful war-beasts. It aims to make mobile pressure and coastal routes matter without reducing these varied peoples to a single stereotype.

[Read the Harad and Umbar design](factions/harad-umbar.md).

## Mordor — Sauron's war machine

Mordor is the fortress-land from which Sauron wages war. Its design centers on expendable Orc forces, vulnerable laborers, siege production, and pressure that grows over time. The counterplay is equally explicit: disrupt the workers, exploit the army's lower individual quality, and avoid letting attrition dictate the battle.

[Read the Mordor design](factions/mordor.md).

## Angmar — the northern shadow

Angmar was a northern realm ruled by the Witch-king and used in the wars against Arnor. Its concept focuses on fear, cold, and temporary supernatural reinforcement. Those powers should create short, answerable moments of pressure rather than a permanent source of free troops.

[Read the Angmar design](factions/angmar.md).
